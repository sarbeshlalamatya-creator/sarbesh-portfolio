The thread

Night sky first, then daylight.

Every page opens in the same purple night, with stars and the odd planet, then drops into white for the reading. Four pages end on the same mint footer. The home page stays in the night far longer and closes on a dawn over the hills, with a stupa and a school.

Pedagogy

A lecture, or a conversation?

The platform page argues for the product before it lists a feature. It sets a lecture against a talk with an expert and asks you to pick. The illustrations carry the argument; the copy only confirms it.

“Learning occurs naturally when teaching is dialogic in nature.”From the platform page
Natural ConversationsStudent-teacher dialogue videos you can pause and rewind.
Shared diagnosticsQuiz answers shared with your school teachers, so the conversation carries on in class.
Online presenceTutor support around the clock, booked ahead or on the spot.

Three names on a loop.

“Our technology” draws IVy, Apollo and Tutor Support as soft blobs joined by a dashed line. The copy says IVy and Apollo were invented for the platform. The page doesn't say what either one does, so I've kept them as names here too.

Contents

Confused, then confident.

The contents page tells the whole pitch in three arches and eleven words. The same girl appears first and last; in between, she's at a book with a teacher. The colours warm from blue to yellow as she gets there.

Two audiences

The same feature, told twice.

Students and teachers each get their own page, and several features appear on both. Where the copy is rewritten for the reader, it changes who does the verb. Pick a feature to compare the two versions.

Teachers get the product itself.

The students page follows its hero with illustrations. The teachers page goes straight to a screen of the product: a dashboard with counts, an activity log and a comparison chart, and later a carousel of more. A teacher's first question is "what will I see?", and this answers it.

Looking back

What I'd tighten next.

The night-to-day frame, the characters and the three arches give the site a voice of its own. These fixes would let the words match it.

  1. Replace the stand-ins.The testimonial is signed John Doe and repeats the hero sentence. The footer blurb is lorem ipsum filler. All three news cards share one date, and the Interactive Videos preview on the home page is an empty frame.
  2. Say what the price buys.Per subject or in total, per month or per year. One line under each price would settle it, and the 20% badge could then match the maths.
  3. Write the teachers page for teachers.Its Group Chat card is word for word the students' version. The dashboard mock shows an IGCSE chemistry class from 2020, which reads oddly on a site for the Nepali curriculum.
