What it isAn online store for laptops, printers, monitors and PC parts

My roleUI/UX design
The route

Seven screens, one order.

Most store comps stop at the product page. This one keeps going: a category in two views, the product, the cart, the order confirmation, tracking, and a PC builder off to the side. Walk the route.

Names and addresses on the order pages are blurred by me

Home

Shelves, then a banner, then shelves.

The home page is seventeen bands long. Product shelves do most of the work, and a full-width brand banner breaks them up every few rows so the scroll never turns into one long grid. Pick a band to jump to it.

Product shelfBanner
Category page

Same shelf, two levels of detail.

The Monitors category comes in a list view and a grid view. The list is for comparing: panel type, refresh rate, brightness, warranty, delivery and EMI. The grid is for browsing: photo, two lines of title, price, discount and rating. I rebuilt three list rows so you can flip them.

The grid version of these three is my rebuild
Product and cart

Four chances to add one more thing.

Extras show up twice on the product page and twice in the cart: a protection plan with a running total, a bought-together bundle, an extended warranty and an installation service. Each one has its own button, so nothing is added for you.

Tracking

Four stops and a log.

The track order page sums a package up in four stops, then lists every event underneath with a date and time. I've rebuilt it so the log grows as you move along the line.

Which log line belongs to which stop is my reading
My orders

Four outcomes, four dots.

Order history gives each outcome a coloured dot: an empty green ring while it's on the way, then solid green, red or orange. The filters on the left use the same four words, so in the rebuild they work.

ORDER STATUS
Build your own PC

Twenty-one parts, six on the line.

The PC builder lists twenty-one part types down the side. Six carry a red star, and those six are exactly the stops on the progress line across the top. Optional parts like the monitor sit in the list but never move the line. Tick the parts.

Looking back

What I'd fill in next.

The structure is all here, from list view to order history. What's left is mostly sample content that needs to line up before it goes to development.

  1. Pair every title with its photo.Grid cards put an HP Spectre title on an MSI laptop and a Toshiba laptop title on a monitor. The MSI product page has a Canon printer's spec table and offers AppleCare+ for iPhone 15. The cart calls a laptop warranty "Complete Camera Protection".
  2. Make the sums add up.The protection plan shows 2,30,999 + 13,000 = 2,33,499, which is the bundle's total. The confirmation says NPR 35,017, the total below it says Rs. 35896, and the four items are Rs 522 each. The cart's add-ons are all priced NPR 2,30,000.
  3. Fix the small copy."Barcode and Lebal", "Advanced Serach" and "Referal". Best Selling Products appears twice on the home page, and the footer is still Lorem Ipsum.
