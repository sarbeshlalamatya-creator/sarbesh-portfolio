The brand

A form, filling up.

The inRol mark is an upright yellow bar with three slanted bars beside it, lime, green and deep green, like lines being filled in on a form. The same bars sit faint and oversized behind the splash. Inside the product, the deep green does the work: primary buttons, progress and the selected step.

New application

Eleven steps, and the first one shapes the rest.

A new application runs down an eleven-step rail, from Programme & Enrolment to Submission, with a progress bar, "Autosave on" and a Save draft button. Step one picks an education level and a programme, and the form below changes to suit. For A Level, a note says it "requires 4 enrolment fields: Specialization, Subjects, Level, Enrolled Year".

The bar reads 9%, 18% and 27% on the three screens, which is the step number out of 11. Steps 4 to 11 aren't in the screens I have.

Student list

Every applicant, and how far along.

48 records, twelve to a page. Each row carries the applicant and their ID, programme and campus, intake, a status chip, a progress bar, fee state and an owner. Above the table, saved views turn common questions into one click, and "Save current view" adds your own.

Progress follows status.

In the sample, every status has one fixed progress value. Laid out in order, it puts a rejected application further along than a submitted one.

Admissions overview

The dashboard, checked against itself.

The overview stacks four KPIs, admission trends, programme mix, a conversion funnel, intake deadlines, a breakdown by programme, pending records, recent applications, tasks and activity. Most of its numbers agree with each other and with the other screens. A few don't.

Worked out from the figures on the screens

    The clock in the file.

    The three intake deadlines count down 15, 29 and 99 days. All three only work from one date, 28 July 2026, and the tasks due 31 Jul and 2 Aug fit it too.

    Contracts

    Write it once, merge it for everyone.

    Enrolment agreements are templates with merge tags like {{student.full_name}} and {{programme.title}}. A row of tags sits under the editor. Beside it is a library with versions, signature counts and approval states, and a version history of who changed what. Preview fills the tags for one student.

    Preview values come from Amara Okoye's row in the student list. Tuition and guardian aren't in any screen, so they stay unfilled.

    Reports

    Ask once, delivered every Monday.

    Reports are saved and scheduled. A Weekly Conversion Digest goes out as a PDF on Mondays at 07:00 to 14 recipients, an Intake Capacity Forecast as Excel daily at 06:30, and Document SLA breaches hourly. A builder underneath works in dimensions, measures and filters, with distribution lists and export formats beside it.

    Looking back

    What I'd tighten next.

    The structure is strong: an application that adapts to the programme, views that answer questions, and contracts built from data. The sample data and a few signals need the same care.

    1. Let one number mean one thing.The KPI says 317 documents pending and the pending-records card says 2. Students enrolled reads 1,284 while the breakdown below totals 95. The header filter says Sept 2026, the breakdown says AUTUMN, and the best admission day is in 2024. Activity moves APP-2026-1113 to Contract Sent, while the list still shows it In Review.
    2. Colour trends by meaning, not direction.Every down arrow is red. A shorter build time, fewer contracts awaiting approval and fewer documents pending are all good news, but they read as warnings.
    3. Make progress honest.Rejected at 45% sits above Submitted at 35%. A rejected application could end its bar or show it differently. Names in the list are clipped mid-word, "Devraj Benne" and "Tomas Kapoo", where the column could wrap or show a tooltip.
