Two sides of one product

Students get two tabs. Staff get the rest.

The dashboard greets staff with "Welcome to IIC Student Attendance System", and the header carries the Islington College crest next to the ING logo. Sign-in takes an email and password, or an Outlook account. Behind it is a long rail of tools for the people who plan classes, sort students into groups and answer for the numbers.

Routine import

Check the spreadsheet before it goes live.

A whole term's timetable arrives as one Excel file, and every attendance figure depends on it. So the planner doesn't take the sheet on trust. It passes through an agreement, a format check and a review before anyone can press Import. Step through it.

Fourteen headers, or nothing.

The failure message names the column headers the system expects. They are the same fourteen columns the review table shows, one for one. Untick a few to see the message my version gives.

The design's message lists all fourteen; naming only the missing ones is my version

Nine issues, one at a time.

A good sheet lands in review with four counts. Each problem then gets its own dialog, with a nine-part progress bar: green for fixed, amber for the current step. The dialog names the row and what's wrong, then offers a fix. Values can be picked from what the system already knows. Modules, groups, rooms and teachers can also be created on the spot. "Apply this action to all 20 similar rows" turns one fix into a batch.

A clash, shown from both sides.

A collision isn't only counted. The Conflicting Routines dialog sets the imported row beside what it clashes with and names the cause. Here it's overlaps in time, room, teacher and group across 2 rows. It checks two places: the routine already in the system, and the rest of the same Excel file, so two rows in one upload can collide with each other. The fields behind the clash are in red.

When does it start?

Before the import is final, every row gets an effective date. Defaults are set per level, alongside a routine batch name. Rows can be ticked in bulk and given a custom date. A preview then lists every date, level by level and row by row, before Confirm Dates & Import.

One import at a time.

If another admin is mid-import, the planner says who and locks the page: "The routine is currently being imported by Aayush Bhakta Pradhananga. All actions are temporarily disabled until the import process is completed." Two people can't load two timetables over each other.

Merge and swap

Rework two classes without retyping either.

Once a routine is live, two actions on the Academic Routine page change classes in pairs. Merge folds two classes into one. Swap trades their times, days and rooms. Both end on a verify step that marks in green what is about to change.

Swap, field by field.

Each row between the two routines has its own swap button, so an admin can trade just the room, or the day and the time together. Try it on the pair from the design.

The two steps as designed. A wider version, below, adds teacher and group rows, a third routine, start and end dates and a required remark, and outlines the fields that will change in teal.

Merge in three steps.

Preview puts the two classes side by side. Update Routine Details sets the merged class: lecturer, module, group, type, day, time and room, with the block filled in from the room and a required remark. Verify shows the result, with the new values in green.

Student grid

A student's whole time at college is a tab.

New students land in an Unsorted list and are assigned to groups from there. Each cohort tab lays its groups side by side, L1C1, L1C2, L1C3, with transfer, graduate and email actions on each. The last four tabs aren't cohorts at all: Graduation, Withdrawn, Intermission and Force Withdrawn hold the students who have left the timetable.

Student information

Every change leaves a note.

A student's record holds their details, attendance and a comment thread. The thread also logs what staff did: "sent this student to Intermission", "transferred from L1C1 to L1C2", each with a name and a time, in a dashed box so it reads apart from people's comments. Try the actions below.

Attendance details

Late counts here too.

The student's attendance tab lists 14 classes and totals them: 5 present, 2 late, 7 absent, and a present rate of 50%. Fifty per cent is 7 of 14, which only works if both late classes count as present, the same rule the student portal follows.

Worked out from the figures on the screen
50%

Two absences carry their own label, "Early Punch-In" and "Late Punch-In". The student did punch in, outside the window, and the class still counts as missed. The detail report agrees with the rule: 5 present and 1 late out of 13 is shown as 46%.

Reports

Summary or detail, one form at a time.

The report page has two forms side by side. Picking one blurs the other, so it's always clear which filters will be used. Both open a grid per group: the summary gives totals per module, and the detail shows every class day.

Contact numbers are blurred in both grids.

Looking back

What I'd tighten next.

The structure holds: a timeline for the day, a review step for imports, tabs for every stage of a student's time, and a log on every record. These are the fixes I'd make next.

  1. Make the sample numbers agree.In a tool built on counting, people check the numbers. The year cards add to 3,865 but the total reads 3,856. Planner rows run 12:30 to 14:00 and say 2.5 hours. A schedule block sitting at 1 pm is labelled 11:00 to 13:00. One report cell reads "7&", and the grid has two IT2 tabs. In the import dialogs, a module missing in 06 rows offers to fix "all 20 similar rows", the level picker is labelled Course, and a conflict card labels the day as Time.
  2. Give red fewer jobs.Red means absent, cancelled, early punch-in, late punch-in and a planner collision. Only some of those need someone to act. A cancelled class is news, and could take a neutral colour.
  3. Put the key on the grid.The detail report leans on ten codes, and LA and EP aren't spelled out anywhere. There's an info icon in the report header, and it should open a legend.
