Case study · Web app · 2024

Certify.

Certificate generation and validation

Design it once, send it to everyone, and let anyone check it's real.

What it isA tool for a college to build certificate templates, issue them in bulk by email, and verify them in public
Shown here15 screens and a component sheet
My roleUI/UX design
The idea

Make, send, prove.

A paper certificate only has to look right. A digital one also has to reach the right person and hold up when someone checks it. The screens fall into those three jobs.

My grouping of the screens
MakeUpload a design, mark where the name goes, write the caption and the email.Editor, template library, component sheet
SendImport recipients, track who got their email, resend or edit.Certificate detail, recipients, email settings
ProveAnyone can paste an ID and see who it was issued to, then share it.Validator, privacy policy
Make · Editor

Four tabs, one button that always says what's next.

The editor keeps the certificate on a large canvas and the work in a narrow panel. A rail of four icons (Upload, Text, Caption, Email) switches the panel, and the blue button at the bottom always names the next step. Walk the chain.

Make · Placeholder

The blank line is the product.

Every certificate here is the same picture with a hole in it. The editor drops a "Sample Text" box in the gap, and the finished sheet fills it with a signature-style name. Type a name to fill the gap yourself.

Sample Text

The design shows "Olivia Thompson" in a script face. Caveat stands in here.

{{name}}{{email}}{{courseName}}{{completionDate}}{{startDate}}{{issued date}}

The caption step lists {{issued date}} with a space. Every other token is one camel-case word.

The four upload states, from the component sheet.

Make · Library

Cards to browse, rows to manage.

The template library opens on cards with a coloured thumbnail and two buttons. The list view trades the thumbnails for the facts an admin acts on: lock status, how many recipients, size and last edit.

Send · Recipients

Three numbers that add up.

A template's detail page leads with its delivery count. Sent plus pending equals the total, so the admin can see at a glance that nothing has slipped through. Below is the recipients table, rebuilt with emails left out.

Prove · Validator

Paste an ID, get an answer.

The public validator needs no login. Paste the ID and you get the certificate, who issued it, who it's for, and a big green stamp. Share buttons sit right under the link. Try it with the sample ID, or with anything else.

Looking back

What I'd tighten next.

The make-send-prove loop is all here, and the editor's next-step button carries it well. These fixes are about trust, which is what a certificate sells.

  1. One ID, one vocabulary.Recipient IDs look like ICKAHKDQQ, but the validator checks a 24-character ID. Placeholders mix {{issued date}} with camelCase. A validator only works if the ID on the page is the one in the table.
  2. Make status honest.Every row is Pending while the card says 1,190 sent. The table says 11 recipients, shows 11 rows and footers "1-10 of 100". Five rows share one ID.
  3. Proofread the labels.Email settings labels the email body "Confirm New Password". The validator spells "Reciepient". "Add New Student" becomes "Add New Awardee" between versions, and the list view still has lorem ipsum descriptions.
