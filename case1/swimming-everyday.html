The idea

Two volumes, one brand.

The academy pages shout. Giant condensed capitals, full-bleed yellow, a cartoon turtle on a surfboard. That's the job: make a parent stop scrolling and think "my kid could learn here".

Then you walk into the gear shop and the volume drops. The background turns cream, the type shrinks to a size you can shop at, and yellow is kept for the one button that moves you forward.

Structure

Every page ends at the pool's edge.

Nine pages, nine different jobs, and the same ending on all of them: a "Ready to join us?" band and the "Learn to swim!" sign-up form. A parent who lands on the gear shop or the facilities gallery is still one scroll from enrolling.

The shared ending: the join band and the sign-up form
Character

The turtle does the wayfinding.

The mascot isn't decoration. Each pose sits next to the thing the page wants you to do, and it gets smaller as the pages get more serious.

The shop

Pick it up where you swim.

The shop has no shipping step. Gear is collected at the Pulchowk or Pokhara branch, so checkout asks for a branch and a pickup time instead of a delivery address.

      Looking back

      What I'd tighten next.

      The two volumes and the shared ending hold the site together. These are the places I'd take another pass.

      1. One form per page.The booking page has its own class form and then the shared "Learn to swim!" form below it. Two identical forms on one page asks people to guess which one counts.
      2. Make the numbers agree.The same stats carry different labels on different pages: "10K+" is a success rate on Home and students trained on About. Each number should mean one thing everywhere.
      3. Design the phone version.Every page here is desktop. Most parents will meet the sign-up form and the checkout on a phone, so those come first.
