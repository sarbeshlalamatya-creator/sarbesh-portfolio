The system

Every page takes a letter's colour.

The KAVYA wordmark is drawn in orange, blue, green and red. Each page header borrows one of them for a slanted band, with a photo cut to the same angle on the right. One shape, five colours. Pick a page.

My reading of the colour choice

The band above is rebuilt in CSS; the screenshot under it is the page's own header.

Why Kavya

Four arches, four promises.

The home page answers "why this school" with four tall columns, each topped with a coloured arch that carries one claim. Photos fill the rest of the column, so the claim and the proof stand together.

Scholarship

The page says 10% of students each year are eligible, with the full college fee waived. Three words that start with the same letter, so the rule is easy to repeat.

Courses

Subjects, then where they lead.

The courses page lists compulsory subjects, then two columns of optional ones, each ending in career tags. The columns aren't titled on the page; the home page calls them Science and Management pathways, so I've used those names. Pick one.

    Looking back

    What I'd tighten next.

    The slanted header and the colour per page give a six-page site a real system. The content inside it needs the same care.

    1. Write each message once.The chairman, the principal and the mission all reuse one paragraph, word for word. The News hero is lorem ipsum, and the Apply hero repeats the Courses line about subject choices.
    2. Check the stories.The alumni article is an Islington College event dated 16 May, held in December, with a subtitle about a festival of colours. The listing shows its two stories four times each.
    3. One contact, one form.Settle the address and phone numbers, label the dropdown that's currently called "Email Address", and drop the footer form on the page that is already a form.
