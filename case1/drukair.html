Home page

Three ways in.

The file holds three home pages. One leads with a promise, one with the country's name, and one with a line about the traveller. All three share the nav, the package block and the footer. Scroll each one and compare.

The file doesn't say which one shipped, so I'm not calling a winner.

Packages

The trip is the product.

Every direction sells the same unit: a named tour with its length in days and nights. In the dark home page they ride in a carousel beside "Special Packages". In the others, one package gets a big photo, a summary, two buttons and a strip of moments from the trip.

Tour detail

Facts before the long read.

Flights

Search first, then ask.

The flights page puts a search bar on top and an inquiry form below it, so a traveller can look up a route and then ask a person. Try the trip type. In the design, Round Trip shows the same three fields; I've added a return date to show what it would need.

Trust

Why this company, said three ways.

The dark home page spends its second half on reasons: visa help and safety under "Why Choose Us", a big "20+ Years" beside the airline connection, guest quotes, and a row of partner logos.

Looking back

What I'd tighten next.

Packages, flights and stays all have a place, and the navy and orange hold them together. What's left is making each page belong to Bhutan.

  1. Put the name in.Every page still says LOGO. The footer calls the company "Drukai Holidays" while the copy above says Drukair.
  2. Swap the stock for the place.The tour gallery and the stays results use suburban houses and apartments. The results header spells Thimphu as "Thimpu".
  3. Make each list Bhutan's.Drop Beach and Beach front, and replace hotel facilities on the tour page with an itinerary. Two guest quotes repeat the same sentence.
